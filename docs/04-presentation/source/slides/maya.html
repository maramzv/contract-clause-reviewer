Maya’s review, start to finish

1

Upload once

The 30 contracts go in together, with all five categories selected.

2

Read the table

Each contract and category is Found with its quotation and page, Not found, review required, or Failed to process.

3

Check the wording

She opens the contract at the quoted page, accepts correct findings and removes false matches.

4

Search the gaps by hand

Only the Not found cells need her own search, because an empty result never proves the clause is absent.

5

Send the table

She downloads the CSV and sends it, with the contracts, to her senior lawyer.

What her senior sees

One row per finding: contract, category, exact quotation, page and review state. A bracketed row marks every category nobody has confirmed.

“Where does the contract actually say that?” is answered by the page number.

He still decides what each clause means. The tool does not interpret.

Maya is an illustrative persona. Time saved is untested: the reviewer pilot was not run.

4

The tool in Maya’s own working day, following the PRD’s first user journey. Her senior sends 30 contracts and asks for five items from each. She uploads them once and reads one table instead of searching 30 files five times. Every Found row has the exact wording and its page, so she can check it quickly. Every gap is visible, and she searches only those by hand. The senior lawyer receives the CSV and the contracts and decides what each clause means. Maya is an illustrative persona and the time saved has not been measured.
