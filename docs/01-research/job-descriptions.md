# Job Descriptions Relevant to This Build

These are the real postings that connect our build (contract clause extraction and review) to actual job duties. They come from section 6 of the final PRD.

**What these postings do and don't show.** They show that reviewing contracts for clauses like assignment and change of control is a real part of these jobs. They do not show how often reviewers miss clauses, how long extraction takes, or whether our tool would save time. Reading what each posting "reveals" about the daily work is our own interpretation.

## Junior corporate associate roles

### Andrews Myers: Corporate Transactional Associate (first-year / junior)

- **What it says (explicit duties):**

## Postings with pain-related language

These three are about turnaround, renewals and escalation. E-Space and LegalShield state goals and duties, not complaints, so they show what reviewers are held to, not how often things go wrong.

### E-Space: Contract Paralegal (also Contract Specialist)

- **Who it targets:** an in-house role. It is not explicitly junior.
- **What it says (explicit duties):**
  - Handle routine contract management and resolve routine matters independently
  - Route non-standard NDAs to Corporate Counsel and escalate non-standard or higher-risk vendor provisions
  - Track key dates, renewal deadlines, expirations and obligations, and flag upcoming action items
  - Track and report on contract volume, turnaround times and pipeline status
- **What it reveals (our reading):** Someone is watching how fast contracts move and has to decide which terms need a senior person.
- **How it supports the build:** It supports the renewal category and the practice of escalating non-standard provisions to a senior lawyer.
- **Limits:** It asks for volume and turnaround to be tracked, and for contracting efficiency to improve. It does not say reviewers struggle to find clauses or that review is slow.
- **Link:** https://jobs.lever.co/espace/59ffb796-535d-4f6e-95db-2146d36953d5

### LegalShield (PPLSI): Paralegal

- **Who it targets:** an in-house commercial contracts role. It is not explicitly junior.
- **What it says (explicit duties):**

  - Help build tools and workflows that improve turnaround time, reduce cycle times and legal exposure, and create consistency in review through checklists

- **What it reveals (our reading):** The team wants reviews to be faster and more consistent, and uses checklists to get there.
- **How it supports the build:** It supports checklist-based review, consistent handling across contracts and renewal tracking.
- **Limits:** It states goals for turnaround and consistency, not a measured problem.
- **Source limit:** The posting was removed on October 20, 2026. The text comes from the Built In copy read on November 4, 2026, and the link may no longer show the full text.

### Pylon: Legal & Contract Ops

- **Who it targets:** an in-house role at a startup in San Francisco. It is not explicitly junior.

- **Limits:** The fit is weak. The volume (10+ reviews a month) is far below a 30-contract diligence batch. It covers customer contracts and redlines, not clause extraction for diligence. No clause types are named.
- **Source limit:** The wording is the same in two aggregator copies (JoinRise and Sonara). JoinRise lists the role as open and posted December 17, 2025. Sonara shows no date. Its status was not confirmed on Pylon's own site.
- **Link:** https://joinrise.co/pylon/legal-contract-ops-i9id

## Other evidence that the problem is real

Besides the postings, two research sources came up: the CUAD paper (including how its data was labeled) and a construction-contract study. **Neither has been verified.** The links they came from were lost, so look up the originals before citing them.

- **The 2021 CUAD paper.** It describes reviewers manually searching hundreds of pages to find whether relevant clauses exist, what they say, and where they appear. This shows that finding clauses is a documented review task with a lot of manual effort.
- **CUAD's annotation process.** It included keyword searches for missed categories and repeated category-by-category checks. This is evidence about how the dataset was labeled, not a measurement of how often everyday reviewers make errors.
- **A construction-contract study.** It interviewed 31 experienced professionals and found demanding tasks beyond classification, including extracting obligations, timelines and payment structures. Extraction can support a larger review workflow, but the findings are specific to construction contracts.

**How the problem could be stated:** reviewers spend time locating and organizing important terms across lengthy contracts. The tool extracts clause types with source quotes so a person can verify them more quickly. The open question is whether the tool saves time after the reviewer has checked its output. CUAD accuracy alone can't answer that.

## What the postings tell us about the build

- **Primary users:** junior corporate associates and corporate contracts paralegals. Andrews Myers anchors the junior role. ImmunityBio gives the clearest clause-level link.
- **When it gets used:** a reviewer receives agreements and a diligence checklist for a deal.
- **Workflow (our proposal, not stated in any posting):** upload contracts, choose clause categories, verify the extracted passages, export a review table.
- **Checklist support:**
  - Assignment and change of control have direct support (ImmunityBio, Cantor Fitzgerald).
  - Renewal has support from E-Space and LegalShield.
  - Governing law and termination for convenience are our own proposed categories. We mapped them to CUAD labels but no posting names them.
- **Human review stays:** the tool finds and organizes wording. Lawyers decide what it means.

## Evidence boundary

The pain points and time-saving targets in the PRD are still hypotheses. These postings link the build to real responsibilities. They do not prove that reviewers miss clauses, that extraction takes a lot of time, or that our tool would save time.
