Contract Clause Extraction and Review

Clautra

Find the clause. Trace the source.

Turn a batch of commercial contracts into a review-ready clause table. Every finding is a verbatim quotation with its page reference, and every gap is flagged for attorney review.

Kerrian Gordon & Mara Munoz

The problem

Checking contracts by hand is slow, and a missed detail is costly

What's going on

When a company is being bought, lawyers read piles of its contracts to find a few key sections, called clauses. For example: what happens to a contract if the company is sold? This careful check is called due diligence. We think the time goes here:

  • Hunting for the same kinds of clauses in every contract
  • Copying quotes and page numbers into a spreadsheet
  • Not knowing if a search that finds nothing means the clause isn't there

These are our best guesses. No job posting proves them yet.

Who it's for

  • Main users: junior lawyers, paralegals (legal assistants) and legal analysts who read the contracts
  • Also: senior lawyers, who check the results and decide what each clause means for the deal
  • Later: contract managers, in-house lawyers, purchasing and legal operations teams
  • Example: Maya, a junior lawyer, must review 30 contracts before a company purchase closes (an illustrative person, not a real customer)

The solution

Upload contracts, get a table you can trust and check

1Uploadyour contracts (PDF or text)
2Choosewhat to look for
3Findthe app pulls out exact quotes
4Checkyou accept or remove each one
5Exporta spreadsheet (CSV) to share

100% Exact quotes

A quote is shown only if those exact words appear in the contract. If the AI rewords something, it is thrown out.

100% No silent gaps

If nothing is found, the app says so. A blank never means "the clause isn't there".

Three results per topic

  • Found: a quote with its page
  • Not found, review required: a person should look
  • Failed to process: e.g. a scanned image it can't read

The app finds and organizes the wording. Lawyers still decide what it means.

How it's built

Six steps behind the scenes, and the fourth protects the reviewer

1Readtext from each PDF page
2Splitinto pieces (~1 page each)
3Findthe AI suggests quotes
4Verifykeep a quote only if it's word for word
5Saveto a small local database
6Exportto a spreadsheet

The five things it looks for

Assignment

Can one side hand the contract to someone else?

Change of control

What happens if the company is sold or merged?

Ending without a reason

Can either side walk away? (termination for convenience)

Renewal

Does the contract renew, and how?

Governing law

Which state's or country's laws apply?

Built with: Streamlit (a tool for web apps), Python, SQLite (a simple local database) and Claude Sonnet (the AI), with a fake offline AI as a backup. It runs on one computer. Test data: we use CUAD, a public set of 510 real contracts that experts already marked up, so we can check our answers. We never use real deal contracts.

How we'll know it works

We test on 102 public contracts we never trained on:

  • Finds at least 80% of the clauses that are really there
  • At least 70% of what it flags is correct, and beats a plain keyword search
  • Reviewers take at least 30% less time than by hand, missing no more
  • The 30 biggest contracts finish in 30 minutes

Risks and where it fits

What could go wrong, and how we're different

What could go wrong

  • Messy PDFs: a quote that doesn't match is dropped and shown as "not found", never as a wrong quote
  • Very long contracts: many AI calls at once, with saved answers so re-runs are free
  • Cost and speed: not measured yet, so we test 3 contracts first and cap spending
  • AI fails during the demo: replay a saved earlier run. The fake AI is the last resort

Similar tools already exist

Litera Kira, Spellbook and Luminance already find clauses, at much bigger scale. We don't compete on features. We test three things:

  • Every quote is checked word for word
  • An explicit "not found, review required" result
  • Accuracy measured on public contracts
